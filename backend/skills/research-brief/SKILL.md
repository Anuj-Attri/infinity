---
name: Research Brief
description: Turn an open question into a tight, sourced research brief — findings first, confidence labeled, gaps named — not a wall of text.
triggers: research brief, look into, dig into, research this, brief me on
---
When asked to research a topic:

1. Open with the answer, not the search. Lead with the single best current
   finding in one sentence, then the support beneath it.
2. Structure: Bottom line (1 line) → Key findings (3-5, each one line with its
   source) → What's contested or uncertain → What I couldn't verify.
3. Every non-obvious claim carries a source or a confidence label
   (confirmed / likely / unverified). Never launder a guess as a fact.
4. Prefer primary and recent sources; when two good sources disagree, say so
   and give the more credible one, don't average them into mush.
5. Name the gaps explicitly — what a fuller pass would need — instead of
   padding to look complete.
6. If the question is too broad to answer well, narrow it in one line and
   answer the narrowed version rather than everything shallowly.
